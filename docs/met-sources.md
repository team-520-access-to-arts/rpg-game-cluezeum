## Fact and fiction

Artwork introductions, artist/date/medium captions, credits and process explanations are sourced facts. Observation questions use visible details checked in the downloaded image; the Le Gray and Richards questions explicitly ask for museum-record information. Interpretation is framed as the player's comparison, never an assertion of an artist's private intentions.

Only the imaginary archive, player's role, mission transitions and endings are fictional. No missing historical objects, fabricated quotations, secret messages from artists, shared historical journey or invented curatorial instructions are presented as facts. The Met Messenger is an invented present-day role, not Joseph Roulin.

## Route logic

Gandy begins every route with a dated observation. Players choose Delacroix or Marville first, then see the other: color versus a photographed city sky. Both lead to Le Gray's documented photographic construction. Players then choose McEntee or Sargent first, then see the other: colored support versus a sky-filled composition. Richards closes every route with a portable graphite study. All routes visit all seven once. The two choices weight the image-making or observation ending, with the later choice breaking ties. Each of four perspectives has its own mission framing and two conclusions.
